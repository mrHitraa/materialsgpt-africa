# Preliminary Literature Evidence Extraction

## Status

The Literature page now contains **27 numeric records from nine supplied papers** plus three qualitative 316L findings. The original 22 numeric records were marked verified based on the team's confirmation; reviewer identity and review date were not supplied. On 2026-11-10, the four Kumar et al. 316L mass-loss values and two qualitative findings were checked against their supplied PDFs. The Gomes et al. abstract supports its stated rate and conditions, but the exposure interval and rate calculation remain unverified. The Sandoval-Amador paper was checked in full; an internal salt-formulation conflict is documented and flagged rather than silently resolved. These are source-backed research observations, not model outputs or service-life predictions.

The records are stored in `src/lib/extracted-observations.ts` and displayed on the Literature page. The supplied PDFs remain in the user's Downloads folder and are not copied into the application or uploaded by it.

## Evidence coverage and comparability

- **Stage 02 status:** The design framework is complete for the team-confirmed scope: IN625, P91, and 316L for the hot-side comparison, with A516 kept separate pending confirmation of its component service temperature. The framework defines condition-matched comparisons and preserves original metrics and units. Weights are deferred; coverage counts are not performance scores, and the app does not rank candidates.
- **Reference-temperature coverage (565 °C):** 5 records from one study: P91 at 1, 12, and 15 days; 304SS at 1 day; and IN625 at 15 days. This is not a balanced or shared time series across the alloys.
- **600 °C isothermal coverage:** 3 records at 4,000 h for IN625, H230, and 740H from one study, plus the 7-day isothermal arms for AISI 321, AISI 347, IN625, and IN825 from a separate study.
- **Thermal cycling:** 4 seven-day records (AISI 321, AISI 347, IN625, IN825) from one study cycling between 250 and 600 °C in argon. The comparator is isothermal at 600 °C, not at the target 565 °C. Cycling also changes the time at peak temperature, so the results do not isolate thermal fatigue.
- **A516:** 6 records at 400 and 450 °C across 0, 0.5, and 1 wt% chloride. All are annualized by the source report from short tests and described there as upper-bound estimates.
- **Initial extraction limitation:** The original 22-record set had no 316L measurement. The follow-up extraction added five 316L numeric results; four are now source-checked and one remains pending. The 304SS evidence is not a substitute for 316L.
- **316L coverage added and checked:** Four Kumar et al. mass-loss values are source-checked. The Gomes et al. abstract directly reports the 8.6 μm/year rate and conditions, but its exact rate interval and derivation remain pending full-text access. Two qualitative findings are source-checked; the Sandoval-Amador full text is checked and explicitly flagged for its conflicting salt formulations. No 316L numeric result is exactly at 565 °C.
- **316L gap:** No extracted numeric result is exactly at 565 °C. There is no 316L thermal-cycling result in the current extracted set. Do not equate wrought and L-PBF material, synthetic-air and nitrogen tests, or nitrite/oxide-modified salts with the reference condition.
- **Study-level diversity:** The 27 numeric records come from nine sources; multiple rows from one paper are not independent studies or independent validation samples. The original 22 user-confirmed records and the five newly extracted, pending 316L numeric records have different verification states.

- The papers contain real measured mass-loss / corrosion results, and the application can now show a traceable subset without treating demo values as scientific data.
- The records are heterogeneous. They vary in temperature, material, duration, cycling, salt impurity, and rate-calculation basis. Do not rank the values as if they were measured under one protocol.
- A simple descriptive comparison within each study and its recorded conditions is possible. Any model fitted to these data would be exploratory and confounded by study and test-condition differences; a leave-one-study-out evaluation would have only four study groups and is not sufficient to establish generalization.
- Thermal-cycling values here are at 600/250 °C, not a validated 565 °C plant cycle, and the paper attributes the difference in part to the reduced time at 600 °C.
- A516 chloride results are valuable for studying chloride sensitivity, but should remain a separate condition group.
- No agreed component failure criterion or independent held-out validation set is available. These records therefore do not support service-life prediction.
